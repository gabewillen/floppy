## The Gap

floppy dropped the original's Environment. Today `bots/floppy/floppy.scx` is the root session, so:

* what the bot puts into the environment goes nowhere: speaking's `environment.sound`, relayed by the communication, is a `<log label="environment.sound">` record ("the root has no parent") plus a fan-out to the bot's own input abilities; every other event the body emits is a log record for the same reason
* `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` reach the bot only as host input on `scx run`'s standard input
* devices are a static `<data id="devices" expr="[]"/>` that `<bot:attention>` and `bot.focus_device` pick from, with a `device_source_refs` map nothing fills; no device runs, and a selected device action has nowhere to go
* no second bot or device can share a space with the bot, hear it, or be heard by it
* the model never sees the original's `<environment id="...">` world block

## The Original's Environment

* **A scope with two planes.** Addressing (every started machine in the scope, device firmware included, stays reachable and can emit into the environment) is separate from presence (the participant set). Only an instance admitted by `join` is a participant, and only participants receive a broadcast. Abilities run in a private scope under their bot: they can emit into the environment but never receive its broadcasts. Device firmware is never a participant; it gets stimuli through its device's forward, so a broadcast reaches it once.
* **Join and leave.** A bot's attach starts it in the environment, joins it (with an optional placement), and dispatches `bot.activate`; its detach dispatches `bot.deactivate` and leaves. A device joins when it starts (with its own placement) and leaves when it stops; a device powers its peripherals first, and each of them joins too. Joining requires a started instance, is idempotent, and refuses an instance started in another environment or one already placed elsewhere.
* **Broadcast.** `broadcast(event, origin)` delivers an `environment.sound` or `environment.visual` to every participant it is still audible to, one delivery per recipient. The emitter says how loud the sound is (`amplitude_db`, dB SPL at 1 m) and the environment says how far it carries: a participant with a placement `{position, threshold_db}` receives the sound at `received_level_db = amplitude_db - 20 log10(max(d, 0.01) / 1.0)` (free-field spreading, -6 dB per doubling; 1 cm clamp so an earpiece at the ear is not a domain error) and hears it only when that level is at least its threshold. With no amplitude, no origin, or no placement there is nothing to measure: the participant hears it and `received_level_db` is left as it was. A visual stimulus has no amplitude, so it reaches every participant. When the filter leaves nobody, nobody receives it (the original guards the case where an empty recipient list would mean "everybody"). The emitter is a participant too, so a bot hears its own speech; its efference copy is what withholds it.
* **Who emits.** Speaking's playout broadcasts `environment.sound` straight into the environment it was started in, with its mouth's (speaker's) amplitude and placement as origin when it has a mouth, and no origin when it has none. A speaker device transduces `devices.audio.output` into `environment.sound`. A phone broadcasts its ring as `environment.sound` (`kind` `phone.ringing`, the caller in the data), and tells the bots holding it about silent facts (a call connecting, ending) with `bot.input` sent straight to them, never to the room.
* **What the bot does with it.** While active, the bot's body fans each `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` it receives out to its input abilities, never to the cognition. A device's `bot.input` reaches the bot with `target_device`, or with a source id the body maps to a configured device reference, which grounds the turn's focus.
* **Devices and actions.** A bot is constructed with its devices (reference to device) and starts them in its environment during activation, as their owner. Nothing on a device checks who sends it an action: owning a device decides whose snapshot shows it, so only its owner is offered its events, but any actor can dispatch to it. On each activation the body declares `owned_devices` (reference to the device's runtime id) on its snapshot. The focused device's enabled transitions (read from its live state machine snapshot) are merged into the bot's snapshot, so the device's events become the turn's choices; a selection naming the device is dispatched straight to it as a real event, and the device's shell delivers it to its firmware.
* **What the model sees.** Each turn the cognition asks the bot's environment for the world block, `Environment.model_snapshot(perspective)` with the bot as perspective: `<environment id="ENV"><self state="..."> ... </self></environment>`. `id` is the environment's own stable identity (never an actor id); `<self>` is the bot's live snapshot, its state as an attribute and its snapshot attributes as children; `owned_devices` resolves each id in the environment's own scope into `<device ref="..." id="..." state="...">` with that device's snapshot attributes (a device not live is dropped; nested mappings become nested elements; `None` and opaque values render nothing). The environment reads every snapshot itself; the cognition invents nothing. The block is the turn's instructions, the system message reasoning and intuition send.
* **Not carried over (and why).** Revival of a cancelled scope, the weak maps, the address registry (`/env/<id>/<actor>`) and studio telemetry are the original's runtime plumbing: SCXML sessions end with their parent and scxml.rs owns telemetry (D14). "Started in another environment" and "placed elsewhere" cannot happen: a session has exactly one invoker and its placement is the environment's data.

### Why There Is No Envelope Event

The first form of this plan relayed a participant's sound as `environment.broadcast {event, data}`. With D104 it is not needed, and it goes:

* telling a participant's sound from host input is not needed: both are a sound in the environment and both are broadcast; the only difference is the origin, and that is read in an `expr` from `_event.invokeid` (a device, the environment's child, has a placement; the host and speaking have none), never in a `cond`
* the origin does not need to travel in the event: a device's placement is the environment's data; speaking has no mouth, so in the original too its sound has no origin; a mouth, when one is ported, is a speaker device with its own placement
* the bot no longer receives its own outward sound from a child (speaking sends past it), so `environment.sound` has one meaning at the body: a sound that reached it; there is nothing to tell apart by `_event.invokeid`

### Why The Bot Asks Once Per Turn

The original computes the block when the turn starts, from live snapshots. SCXML has no synchronous call into another session, so the bot asks and waits: `processing` entry sends `environment.model_snapshot` to `#_parent`, and the turn's attention, choices and cognition input move from `processing`'s `<onentry>` to its transition on the answer (targetless, so the turn's state is unchanged; the turn's `processing_timeout_ms` timer, armed at entry, bounds the wait). One name serves both directions, one meaning on each side (the environment's request, the bot's answer); its schema is `{world?, owned_devices?}`. Nothing is pushed or cached: a block computed on another event would miss a device that changed state on a holder's direct send. The bot's `devices` data is filled from the answer's `owned_devices` keys, so the bot takes no `devices` param.

## scxml.rs Changes

Two changes, each upstream first in `~/VSCode/stateforward/scxml.rs-services` on `feat/invoke-services`, per scxml.rs/AGENTS.md, through its own review and proof; then `git subtree pull --prefix scxml.rs <scxml.rs> feat/invoke-services --squash` here, and the subtree table in docs/decisions.md updated.

### Session Reach (D104)

`#_scxml_<sessionid>` reaches any session of the same run, as SCXML 1.0 C.1 lets the platform decide ("The set of SCXML sessions that are accessible to a given SCXML Processor is platform-dependent"); a session id that names no running session of the run raises `error.communication` ("If the sending SCXML session specifies a session that does not exist or is inaccessible, the SCXML Processor MUST place the error error.communication on the internal event queue"). Nothing else changes: `#_parent` and `#_<invokeid>` keep their meaning, `_event.origin` stays the sender's `#_scxml_<sessionid>` and `_event.invokeid` is set only for an event from an invoked child (SCXML 5.10.1). No new term beyond the spec's: the delivery class is `session`.

* where: `components/scxml-ioprocessor-scxml` (`classify_target`: a well-formed `#_scxml_` id that is not the sender, its parent, or its invocation is a run session, else unreachable), the I/O processor WIT's `send-target`, `dispatch-decision` and `due-route` (a `session` case, at `@0.2.0`, unreleased, no bump), the engine's `SendTarget`/`Dispatch`/`DueRoute`, the Wasmtime host's run (deliver to the named session's external queue, refuse an ended one as `error.communication`), the browser host (parity), the record and replay log (a delivery between sessions is recorded as one), docs/limits.md ("Sessions `#_scxml_<sessionid>` reaches"), and the conformance baselines if any IRP case's result moves (none should: the IRP's `#_scxml_` cases use the sender's own session)

### Reading Another Session's Snapshot (D109)

The original reads a device's and the bot's live state machine snapshots directly. scxml.rs already lets an extension component read the snapshot of the session executing it (`session-snapshot-host.snapshot(exclude)`, executable-content point); this change lets it read any other session of the same run, and adds one element so a document can use it.

* **The host function.** `session-snapshot-host.snapshot` gains `target: option<string>`: none is today's behavior (the executing session, its executable-content image); `some(t)` names another session by an SCXML target, resolved exactly as the I/O processor resolves a `<send target>` after D104 (`#_parent`, `#_<invokeid>` of a live child, `#_scxml_<sessionid>` of any running session of the run). The document is that session at its last checkpoint: the executor runs one session at a time, so every other session is between machine calls and stable (session-snapshot.md, "When a snapshot can be taken"), and the document says `options.point: "checkpoint"`; `invocations` and `previews` are excluded as in the executable-content snapshot, trace carriers stripped. A target that names the executing session is today's image. Errors: `unavailable` for a target that is malformed, names no running session of the run, or names a `failed` or `shut-down` one (the checkpoint document's `status-invalid`); the existing bounds (64 calls per `execute`, 32 MiB) apply unchanged. WIT stays at `@0.2.0` (unreleased; the decision namespace's call passes `none`).
* **The element.** `<scx:snapshot location target exclude?>` in scxml.rs's `scx` namespace (embedded in `scx`, beside `<scx:validate>`): evaluates `target` (an expression, like `<send targetexpr>`), reads that session's snapshot through the host function with the named sections excluded (`exclude`: a space-separated list of the WIT `section` names), and assigns the parsed document to `location`; a failure raises `error.execution` in the reader, as any failing extension element does. It writes, never restores. This is the first time an SCXML document sees a snapshot (docs/executable-content-extensions.md says documents never do); the change says so there.
* **What it may read.** Only sessions of the same run, by the same target forms a `<send>` may use; nothing of another run, the host, or a finished session.
* **Record and replay.** Nothing new is recorded: the read is a pure function of the run's state at the call, and replay reproduces that state (the same inputs, the same cooperative order, the same logical clock), so it reproduces the read, as it already reproduces the executable-content snapshot, which records nothing. The read perturbs nothing in the target session (the existing non-perturbation proof covers the snapshot exports); the reader's data changes only through the element's assignment, which the trace already records.
* **`<decision:choice target>`.** In floppy's decision namespace (not scxml.rs): the optional `target` attribute reads the choices from that session's snapshot instead of the executing one's, through the same host function.
* where: `crates/scxml-wit/wit/scxml/scxml.wit` (`session-snapshot-host`), the Wasmtime host (`hosts/scxml-host-wasmtime/src/session_host/snapshot.rs`, `extension.rs`), the browser host (parity), `namespaces/scx` (the element, its schema and manifest digests), docs/session-snapshot.md and docs/executable-content-extensions.md, the components that call `snapshot` (the extension example)

Still to verify in the environment slice (not known gaps): a `<send target="#_<id>">` in the same macrostep that enters the invoking state raises `error.communication`, which the environment must not take as a leave; a child's `<log>` records keep their `label` with `parent` set.

Limits that stay (SCXML itself, no change proposed): invokes are declared in the document, so an environment admits only the participants it names (`srcexpr` makes a source dynamic, not the number of children); each environment is authored for its scene (the builder can author one).

## Decisions To Add

The maintainer decided these on 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13; D101 is the latest, so they are D102 to D109.

* D102: a bot always runs under an environment: the environment is an SCXML document run as the root session (`environments/<name>/<name>.scx`) whose invoked children are its participants; join is an invoke starting, leave is the invoke ending (cancelled, done, or failed); a bot's abilities are never participants (the original's private scope). There is no bot-as-root path: the body sends every event it emits to `#_parent`, and the environment, the root, logs them. The run command is `scx run ... environments/<name>/<name>.scx`.
* D103: speaking puts its sound straight into the environment (`environment.sound` to `#_scxml_<environment>`), and a device to its parent; the environment delivers every `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` it receives to each participant that perceives it, the emitter included, with the original's free-field law (1 m reference, 1 cm clamp), threshold and `received_level_db`, the origin being the sender's placement when the sender is a placed child (read from `_event.invokeid` in an `expr`). No envelope event: host and participant sounds are both broadcast, and the origin needs no field.
* D104: scxml.rs reaches any session of the same run by `#_scxml_<sessionid>` and raises `error.communication` for any other id (SCXML 1.0 C.1 leaves the set to the platform); done upstream first on `feat/invoke-services`, with no term beyond the spec's.
* D105: devices are SCXML documents the environment invokes (`devices/<name>/<name>.scx`); any real I/O a device does is an invoked service inside it (AGENTS.md's devices line is amended; "I/O must be a service" stays). `holdings` says which bot holds which device: a holder sees the device in `owned_devices`, is offered its choices while focused on it, and receives its `bot.input` with `target_device` stamped. A bot sends a device action straight to the device's address. A device does not check who sends it an action, as the original's devices do not: anyone in the room can act on it (D81 stays absolute).
* D106: the environment's id is the root's `_sessionid` (`scx run --session-id`, default `session`); there is no separate id. The environment builds the world block, as the original's `model_snapshot(perspective)` does: on each turn the bot sends `environment.model_snapshot` to its parent and waits (bounded by the turn's timer); the environment reads the bot's and its held devices' snapshots (D109), composes `<environment id><self state><owned_devices><device ref id state>...` in `room.js` with the original's rendering rules, and answers `environment.model_snapshot {world, owned_devices}`; the bot sends `world` on `bot.ability.cognition.input`, and reasoning and intuition put it in their system instructions.
* D107: a bot activates on join: its activation settings are its `<invoke>`'s `<param>`s (`model`, `bounds`, `database`, `environment`), and the body starts in `live`; the environment sends no `bot.activate`. Since nothing can activate the bot again, `inactive` becomes the bot's top-level `<final>`: a failed activation and `bot.deactivate` end the session there, its leave; rejoining is the environment invoking it again (the original's detach and a new attach).
* D108: host input enters the environment and is routed by what its payload already says: `bot.ability.communication.input` to each participant its `target_ids` names (`targetexpr`), to every bot when empty; untargeted `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` are broadcast; `bot.deactivate` goes to every bot. `bot.input` has one transition: from a device, to that device's holders with `target_device` stamped; from the host, to every bot; the recipients are chosen in an `expr` from `_event.invokeid`. No envelope event and no scxml.rs input change.
* D109: scxml.rs lets a document read the snapshot of another session of the same run: `session-snapshot-host.snapshot` takes an optional SCXML target, resolved as a `<send>` target is, and returns that session's canonical document at its last checkpoint; `<scx:snapshot location target exclude?>` assigns it to a location; nothing new is recorded, since replay reproduces the run's state and so the read. floppy's `<decision:choice>` gains `target`, so a device's choices are its enabled transitions with `event:schema`, read from its snapshot like the body's own. Upstream first on `feat/invoke-services`.

## What The Decisions Changed Or Broke

* `bot.activate` leaves the body and `inactive` becomes final, so a bot is never re-activated in place; every test that sends `bot.activate` (with `model` or `bounds`) moves its settings into the environment launcher's `<param>`s
* the body's emitted events leave its own log for the environment's; a test reading a label across two bots filters by the record's `session`
* host `bot.input` and `bot.deactivate` carry no target, so they reach every bot in a multi-bot environment; a test that needs one bot uses a one-bot environment, or a device's `bot.input`
* `target_ids` now names environment participants (their invoke ids); the slice checks what the conversation does with a `target_ids` that names the bot itself (it reads `target_ids` today as the addressee)
* every turn now waits for one exchange with the environment before it reaches the cognition; a turn whose answer never comes fails at `processing_timeout_ms`, as a turn whose cognition never answers does
* SCXML documents now see snapshots (D109); scxml.rs's documentation said they never do, and the upstream change has to say why that is now right (read-only, same run, the original's model)
* the scxml.rs subtree pull for D104 and D109 also brings whatever else `feat/invoke-services` holds by then
