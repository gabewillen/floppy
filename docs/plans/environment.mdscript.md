## The Gap

floppy dropped the original's Environment. Today `bots/floppy/floppy.scx` is the root session, so:

* what the bot puts into the environment goes nowhere: speaking's `environment.sound`, relayed by the communication, is a `<log label="environment.sound">` record ("the root has no parent") plus a fan-out to the bot's own input abilities; every other event the body emits is a log record for the same reason
* `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` reach the bot only as host input on `scx run`'s standard input
* devices are a static `<data id="devices" expr="[]"/>` that `<bot:attention>` and `bot.focus_device` pick from, with a `device_source_refs` map nothing fills; no device runs, and a selected device action has nowhere to go
* no second bot or device can share a space with the bot, hear it, or be heard by it
* the model never sees the original's `<environment id="...">` block

## The Original's Environment

* **A scope with two planes.** Addressing (every started machine in the scope, device firmware included, stays reachable and can emit into the environment) is separate from presence (the participant set). Only an instance admitted by `join` is a participant, and only participants receive a broadcast. Abilities run in a private scope under their bot: they can emit into the environment but never receive its broadcasts. Device firmware is never a participant; it gets stimuli through its device's forward, so a broadcast reaches it once.
* **Join and leave.** A bot's attach starts it in the environment, joins it (with an optional placement), and dispatches `bot.activate`; its detach dispatches `bot.deactivate` and leaves. A device joins when it starts (with its own placement) and leaves when it stops; a device powers its peripherals first, and each of them joins too. Joining requires a started instance, is idempotent, and refuses an instance started in another environment or one already placed elsewhere.
* **Broadcast.** `broadcast(event, origin)` delivers an `environment.sound` or `environment.visual` to every participant it is still audible to, one delivery per recipient. The emitter says how loud the sound is (`amplitude_db`, dB SPL at 1 m) and the environment says how far it carries: a participant with a placement `{position, threshold_db}` receives the sound at `received_level_db = amplitude_db - 20 log10(max(d, 0.01) / 1.0)` (free-field spreading, -6 dB per doubling; 1 cm clamp so an earpiece at the ear is not a domain error) and hears it only when that level is at least its threshold. With no amplitude, no origin, or no placement there is nothing to measure: the participant hears it and `received_level_db` is left as it was. A visual stimulus has no amplitude, so it reaches every participant. When the filter leaves nobody, nobody receives it (the original guards the case where an empty recipient list would mean "everybody"). The emitter is a participant too, so a bot hears its own speech; its efference copy is what withholds it.
* **Who emits.** Speaking's playout broadcasts `environment.sound` straight into the environment it was started in, with its mouth's (speaker's) amplitude and placement as origin when it has a mouth, and no origin when it has none. A speaker device transduces `devices.audio.output` into `environment.sound`. A phone broadcasts its ring as `environment.sound` (`kind` `phone.ringing`, the caller in the data), and tells the bots holding it about silent facts (a call connecting, ending) with `bot.input` sent straight to them, never to the room.
* **What the bot does with it.** While active, the bot's body fans each `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` it receives out to its input abilities, never to the cognition. A device's `bot.input` reaches the bot with `target_device`, or with a source id the body maps to a configured device reference, which grounds the turn's focus.
* **Devices and actions.** A bot is constructed with its devices (reference to device) and starts them in its environment during activation, as their owner. Nothing on a device checks who sends it an action: owning a device decides whose snapshot shows it, so only its owner is offered its events, but any actor can dispatch to it. On each activation the body declares `owned_devices` (reference to the device's runtime id) on its snapshot. The focused device's enabled transitions (read from its live state machine snapshot) are merged into the bot's snapshot, so the device's events become the turn's choices; a selection naming the device is dispatched straight to it as a real event, and the device's shell delivers it to its firmware.
* **What the model sees.** Each turn the cognition renders the environment block from the bot's perspective (`Environment.model_snapshot(perspective)`): `<environment id="ENV"><self state="..."> ... </self></environment>`. `id` is the environment's own stable identity (never an actor id); `<self>` is the bot's live snapshot, its state as an attribute and its snapshot attributes as children; `owned_devices` resolves each id into `<device ref="..." id="..." state="...">` with that device's snapshot attributes (a device not live is dropped; nested mappings become nested elements; `None` and opaque values render nothing). The block is the turn's instructions, the system message reasoning and intuition send.
* **Not carried over (and why).** Revival of a cancelled scope, the weak maps, the address registry (`/env/<id>/<actor>`) and studio telemetry are the original's runtime plumbing: SCXML sessions end with their parent and scxml.rs owns telemetry (D14). "Started in another environment" and "placed elsewhere" cannot happen: a session has exactly one invoker and its placement is the environment's data.

### One Name, Two Values: `environment`

The bot's `environment` param (and data) is the environment's id, which is also its address; the `environment` field of `bot.ability.cognition.input` is the structured data reasoning renders as the block. They live in different documents and never meet: the body holds the id and builds the field from it; the cognition, reasoning and intuition receive only the field (they never address the environment), and the communication and speaking receive only the id. This adds no name. The alternative, a second name for one of them, is not needed.

### Why There Is No Envelope Event

* telling a participant's sound from host input is not needed: both are a sound in the environment and both are broadcast; the only difference is the origin, and that is read in an `expr` from `_event.invokeid` (a device, the environment's child, has a placement; the host and speaking have none), never in a `cond`
* the origin does not need to travel in the event: a device's placement is the environment's data; speaking has no mouth, so in the original too its sound has no origin; a mouth, when one is ported, is a speaker device with its own placement
* the bot no longer receives its own outward sound from a child (the communication sends speaking's sound past it, D103 amended), so `environment.sound` has one meaning at the body: a sound that reached it; there is nothing to tell apart by `_event.invokeid`

### Why A Device's Address Is Known Before It Starts

A bot needs its devices' addresses (`owned_devices`) to read them and act on them. SCXML gives an invoker the invoke id, not the child's session id, and today scxml.rs names a child `<root id>.<n>` in start order, which the environment cannot know before the child runs. With D110 a child's `_sessionid` is `<parent _sessionid>.<invokeid>`: the environment picks its devices' invoke ids, so it computes each device's address in an `expr` and passes it to its bots in their invoke params at once, with no wait, no self-sent event and no second state. A device the environment invokes again under the same invoke id keeps its address, so the bots that hold it read it and act on it as before; between the old session's end and the new one's start a read finds nothing (the device is dropped from that turn's block) and an action raises `error.communication` in the bot.

An invoke id that repeats within one parent while the earlier child still runs cannot come from a valid document. SCXML 6.4.1 makes an invoke's `id` "the identifier for this instance of `<invoke>`", and a generated one has "the form stateid.platformid" where "platformid MUST be unique within the current session". A literal `id` names one `<invoke>` element, and that element has at most one running instance: leaving its state cancels it before a later entry starts a new one at the end of that macrostep (SCXML 6.4). So a repeat while the earlier child runs means two `<invoke>` elements share a literal `id`, or a generated id that is not unique. scxml.rs already refuses that today as a run failure (`duplicate-invocation`, a session registering an invoke id one of its live invocations has). Under D110 two such children would also share a `_sessionid`, so the same refusal covers it and nothing new is needed. A repeat after the earlier child has ended is the restart above.

## scxml.rs Changes

Two slices, each upstream first in `~/VSCode/stateforward/scxml.rs-services` on `feat/invoke-services`, per scxml.rs/AGENTS.md and docs/handoff.md's merge policy, through its own review and proof; the second slice carries two changes, the snapshot target (D109) and child session ids (D110); then `git subtree pull --prefix scxml.rs <scxml.rs> feat/invoke-services --squash` here, and the subtree table in docs/decisions.md updated.

### Session Reach (D104)

`#_scxml_<sessionid>` reaches any session of the same run, as SCXML 1.0 C.1 lets the platform decide ("The set of SCXML sessions that are accessible to a given SCXML Processor is platform-dependent"); a session id that names no running session of the run raises `error.communication` ("If the sending SCXML session specifies a session that does not exist or is inaccessible, the SCXML Processor MUST place the error error.communication on the internal event queue"). Nothing else changes: `#_parent` and `#_<invokeid>` keep their meaning, `_event.origin` stays the sender's `#_scxml_<sessionid>` and `_event.invokeid` is set only for an event from an invoked child (SCXML 5.10.1). No new term beyond the spec's: the delivery class is `session`.

* where: `components/scxml-ioprocessor-scxml` (`classify_target`: a well-formed `#_scxml_` id that is not the sender, its parent, or its invocation is a run session, else unreachable), the I/O processor WIT's `send-target`, `dispatch-decision` and `due-route` (a `session` case, at `@0.2.0`, unreleased, no bump), the engine's `SendTarget`/`Dispatch`/`DueRoute`, the Wasmtime host's run (deliver to the named session's external queue, refuse an ended one as `error.communication`), the browser host (parity), the record and replay log (a delivery between sessions is recorded as one), docs/limits.md ("Sessions `#_scxml_<sessionid>` reaches"), and the conformance baselines if any IRP case's result moves (none should: the IRP's `#_scxml_` cases use the sender's own session)

### A Snapshot Target (D109)

`session-snapshot-host.snapshot(exclude)` gains one optional parameter, `target`, and nothing else changes: no element, no new term.

* none: today's behavior, the executing session's executable-content image
* `some(t)`: `t` is resolved as a `<send>` target is after D104 (`#_parent`, `#_<invokeid>` of a live child, `#_scxml_<sessionid>` of any running session of the same run); the result is that session's canonical `scxml.session-snapshot/v2` document at its last checkpoint (the executor runs one session at a time, so every other session is between machine calls and stable: session-snapshot.md, "When a snapshot can be taken"), with `options.point` `checkpoint`, `invocations` and `previews` excluded and trace carriers stripped, as in the executable-content snapshot; a target that names the executing session is today's image
* errors: `unavailable` for a target that is malformed, names no running session of the run, or names a `failed` or `shut-down` one; the existing bounds (64 calls per `execute`, 32 MiB) apply unchanged
* only sessions of the same run, by the target forms a `<send>` may use; nothing of another run, the host, or a finished session
* record and replay: nothing new is recorded; the read is a pure function of the run's state at the call, which replay reproduces (the same inputs, the same cooperative order, the same logical clock), so it reproduces the read, as it already reproduces the executable-content snapshot, which records nothing; the read perturbs nothing in the target session (the existing non-perturbation proof covers the snapshot exports)
* WIT stays at `@0.2.0` (unreleased); the existing callers (floppy's decision namespace, the extension example) pass none
* where: `crates/scxml-wit/wit/scxml/scxml.wit` (`session-snapshot-host`), the Wasmtime host (`hosts/scxml-host-wasmtime/src/session_host/snapshot.rs`, `extension.rs`), the browser host (parity), docs/session-snapshot.md and docs/executable-content-extensions.md (an extension component may now read another session of its run; the document still never sees a snapshot itself, only what an extension writes from it)

### Child Session Ids (D110)

A child's `_sessionid` becomes `<parent _sessionid>.<invokeid>` (today `<root id>.<n>`, `n` counting the run's starts), and nothing else changes: the root's id is still the host's (`scx run --session-id`, default `session`), and `_event.origin`, `#_scxml_<sessionid>` and every record that names a session carry the new form. The id is a function of the invoke chain, so a parent knows a child's address before it starts, and a child invoked again under the same invoke id has the same address. Ids stay unique within the run: invoke ids are unique among one parent's running children ([Why A Device's Address Is Known Before It Starts](#why-a-devices-address-is-known-before-it-starts)), and a `.` joins them.

* bound: the id now grows with the nesting depth, so the claim in docs/limits.md that it does not goes; the slice states and tests what an invoke does whose child id would exceed the 1024-byte name bound
* generated invoke ids (approved 2026-10-14): today `<state id>.<invoking session's _sessionid>.<n>`, which under D110 would put the parent's id into its child's id twice; they become `<state id>.<n>`, `n` unique within the invoking session (SCXML 6.4.1's `stateid.platformid`), since the child's address already carries the parent. Every `_event.invokeid`, `done.invoke.<id>`, `error.communication.<id>`, `#_<invokeid>` and `idlocation` value of a generated invoke changes shape with it; a literal `id` does not change. A generated id within a session is no longer unique across the run, only the `_sessionid` it ends is
* record and replay: ids are a pure function of the run, so replay reproduces them as it reproduces today's; the replay log's `path` records stay the invoke-id path from the root
* where: the scheduler's session naming (`crates/scxml-scheduler`), the engine's generated invoke id (`crates/scxml-engine`), the Wasmtime host and the browser host (parity), docs/limits.md ("`_sessionid` of a session": both forms and the depth claim), `hosts/scxml-host-browser/README.md` (its `<root _sessionid>.<n>` line), every test and conformance baseline that asserts a `<root id>.<n>` session id or a `<state id>.<session id>.<n>` invoke id (today `crates/scxml-engine/tests/interpret.rs`'s `s.session.0` cases, including its two 1024-byte bound cases, whose arithmetic assumes `.session.1`, and the generated-lineage comment and assertions in `hosts/scxml-host-wasmtime/tests/basichttp_network.rs`; `rg` at the slice's start for the rest)
* red tests (in scxml.rs, with its own conventions): a child and a grandchild see `_sessionid` `<parent>.<invokeid>`; a sibling reaches a child by that address before the child has sent anything; a child cancelled and invoked again under the same id has the same `_sessionid` and the address reaches the new session; a generated invoke id is `<state id>.<n>` (`s.0`, then `s.1` for the session's next one, the same in a child session as in the root) and gives the child `<parent>.<state id>.<n>`; `idlocation`, `_event.invokeid`, `done.invoke.<id>` and `#_<invokeid>` carry that form; the bound cases re-derived for the shorter form: a state id whose `<state id>.<n>` would exceed the 1024-byte bound still raises `error.execution`; two live children under one id are still refused as `duplicate-invocation`; record and replay reproduce the ids; the browser host does the same

Still to verify in the environment slice (not known gaps): a bot's first device read on `processing` entry finds its devices running when the device invokes come first in the state's document order; a `<send target="#_<id>">` in the same macrostep that enters the invoking state raises `error.communication`, which the environment must not take as a leave; a child's `<log>` records keep their `label` with `parent` set.

Limits that stay (SCXML itself, no change proposed): invokes are declared in the document, so an environment admits only the participants it names (`srcexpr` makes a source dynamic, not the number of children); each environment is authored for its scene (the builder can author one).

## Decisions To Add

The maintainer decided these on 2026-10-12, 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14; D101 is the latest, so they are D102 to D110.

* D102: a bot always runs under an environment: the environment is an SCXML document run as the root session (`environments/<name>/<name>.scx`) whose invoked children are its participants; join is an invoke starting, leave is the invoke ending (cancelled, done, or failed); a bot's abilities are never participants (the original's private scope). The environment keeps membership, holdings, placement and sound. There is no bot-as-root path: the body sends every event it emits to `#_parent`, and the environment, the root, logs them. The run command is `scx run ... environments/<name>/<name>.scx`.
* D103: speaking's sound goes into the environment (`environment.sound` to `#_scxml_<environment>`), and a device's to its parent; amended 2026-10-15: speaking sends it to `#_parent` and the communication passes it on to `#_scxml_<environment>` right after the efference copy to the body, because the copy must reach the listening before the sound (sent by speaking straight to the environment, the sound overtook the copy); the environment delivers every `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` it receives to each participant that perceives it, the emitter included, with the original's free-field law (1 m reference, 1 cm clamp), threshold and `received_level_db`, the origin being the sender's placement when the sender is a placed child (read from `_event.invokeid` in an `expr`). No envelope event: host and participant sounds are both broadcast, and the origin needs no field.
* D104: scxml.rs reaches any session of the same run by `#_scxml_<sessionid>` and raises `error.communication` for any other id (SCXML 1.0 C.1 leaves the set to the platform); done upstream first on `feat/invoke-services`, with no term beyond the spec's.
* D105: devices are SCXML documents the environment invokes (`devices/<name>/<name>.scx`); any real I/O a device does is an invoked service inside it (AGENTS.md's devices line is amended; "I/O must be a service" stays). `holdings` says which bot holds which device: the environment invokes its devices and bots together and gives each bot `owned_devices` (reference to `_sessionid`, the original's map), computed from the devices' invoke ids (D110); a holder shows the device in its environment block, is offered its events as choices while focused on it, and receives its `bot.input` with `target_device` stamped. A bot sends a device action straight to the device's address. A device does not check who sends it an action, as the original's devices do not: anyone in the room can act on it (D81 stays absolute).
* D106: the environment's id is the root's `_sessionid` (`scx run --session-id`, default `session`); there is no separate id. At turn start the bot reads each held device with `<device:snapshot>` and sends the environment's id, its own state and `owned_devices`, and those reads as structured data in `environment` on `bot.ability.cognition.input`; reasoning's existing prompt renderer (D99, objects and arrays as XML elements) produces the environment block, which may not match the original's tag for tag, and intuition puts it in its decision instructions. There is no rendering code in `floppy.js`. The bot's `environment` param (the id) and that field (the data the block is rendered from) share the name: they never meet in one document. The block is always called the environment block.
* D107: a bot activates on join: its activation settings are its `<invoke>`'s `<param>`s (`model`, `bounds`, `database`, `environment`, `owned_devices`), and the body starts in `live`; the environment sends no `bot.activate`. Since nothing can activate the bot again, `inactive` becomes the bot's top-level `<final>`: a failed activation and `bot.deactivate` end the session there, its leave; rejoining is the environment invoking it again (the original's detach and a new attach).
* D108: host input enters the environment and is routed by what its payload already says: `bot.ability.communication.input` to each participant its `target_ids` names (`targetexpr`), to every bot when empty; untargeted `environment.sound` and `environment.visual` are broadcast; `bot.deactivate` goes to every bot. `bot.input` has one transition: from a device, to that device's holders with `target_device` stamped; from the host, to every bot; the recipients are chosen in an `expr` from `_event.invokeid`. No envelope event and no scxml.rs input change.
* D109: a device's state and events are read from its live session, as the original reads a device's state machine snapshot: scxml.rs's `session-snapshot-host.snapshot` takes an optional `target`, resolved as a `<send>` target is and limited to sessions of the same run, and returns that session's canonical document at its last checkpoint (nothing new recorded: replay reproduces the run's state and so the read); the new floppy namespace `device` (an extension component on scxml.rs's existing extension world, no new WIT) has `<device:snapshot location target>`, which assigns `{id, state, attributes, events}` directly to the `location` the bot names, `events` being the event names the device's active transitions accept; the bot turns them into its own choices as it does its own events. No scxml.rs element and no other scxml.rs term. Upstream first on `feat/invoke-services`, after D104, in one slice with D110. Amended 2026-10-16 (the maintainer): a target no running session of the run answers is nothing to read, so `<device:snapshot>` assigns nothing and raises nothing (a malformed element is still `error.execution`), and the bot reads all its held devices in one `<foreach>`: an unreachable read had raised `error.execution`, which ended the read block and dropped every device after the first one not running.
* D110: scxml.rs names a child session `<parent _sessionid>.<invokeid>` (today `<root id>.<n>`), so an invoker knows a child's address before it starts and a child invoked again under the same invoke id keeps it; the environment passes its devices' addresses to its bots in their invoke params at once. A generated invoke id becomes `<state id>.<n>` (today `<state id>.<invoking session's _sessionid>.<n>`), `n` unique within the invoking session, since the child's address already carries the parent; every `_event.invokeid` of a generated invoke changes shape with it. An invoke id that repeats within one parent while the earlier child runs is already refused as `duplicate-invocation` (a literal `id` names one `<invoke>` instance, SCXML 6.4.1), and the same refusal covers the shared session id. Upstream first on `feat/invoke-services`, in the snapshot target's slice.

## What The Decisions Changed Or Broke

* `bot.activate` leaves the body and `inactive` becomes final, so a bot is never re-activated in place; every test that sends `bot.activate` (with `model` or `bounds`) moves its settings into the environment launcher's `<param>`s
* the body's emitted events leave its own log for the environment's; a test reading a label across two bots filters by the record's `session`
* host `bot.input` and `bot.deactivate` carry no target, so they reach every bot in a multi-bot environment; a test that needs one bot uses a one-bot environment, or a device's `bot.input`
* `target_ids` now names environment participants (their invoke ids); the slice checks what the conversation does with a `target_ids` that names the bot itself (it reads `target_ids` today as the addressee)
* every child session id of a run changes form (D110); a floppy test that asserts a `<root id>.<n>` id moves to `<parent>.<invokeid>`; a device re-invoked under the same invoke id keeps its address, so its holders keep it ([Why A Device's Address Is Known Before It Starts](#why-a-devices-address-is-known-before-it-starts))
* the bot's turn now reads every held device's snapshot on `processing` entry, synchronously, in executable content (64 reads per element execution is scxml.rs's bound; one element per device)
* the scxml.rs subtree pull for D104 and D109 also brings whatever else `feat/invoke-services` holds by then
